# Sandesh Vairagar — The Atlas of Becoming
## Portfolio creative direction & experience brief

**Status:** Creative direction only. This document does not authorize or include website implementation.

**Purpose:** Establish an original, authentic and technically credible visual direction for Sandesh Vairagar’s future portfolio. It should help a recruiter understand the person, the work and the next opportunity within a few minutes, while rewarding visitors who explore further.

## 1. The central idea

### THE ATLAS OF BECOMING

**Tagline:** *An evolving journey through creativity, engineering and intelligence.*

An atlas is a collection of maps made by observing, testing and revising. That makes it a fitting metaphor for an engineering student whose practice is actively taking shape: study a problem, build a model, connect it to a usable product, learn from the result, and continue. The site becomes a map of that process rather than a claim that the journey is complete.

The visual metaphor is a **field atlas / architect’s notebook**: measured grids, route lines, coordinates, material swatches, image plates, restrained annotations and occasional full-bleed cinematic photographs. The portfolio moves from a human point of origin to the systems being explored, then to the next destination. The atlas should feel contemporary and tactile, with a subtle Indian context in the photography, landscape and lived details—not decorative motifs pasted onto a generic tech theme.

### Positioning

> **Aspiring Generative AI Engineer · AI/ML Developer · Full-Stack Developer**

> *Building intelligent applications at the intersection of Generative AI, machine learning and software engineering.*

Keep “aspiring” or equivalent language in the opening positioning. Sandesh is a third-year CSE (Artificial Intelligence) student with project and internship experience, not an established senior engineer or studio. Emphasize practical building, explainable systems, curiosity and growth. His primary direction is Generative AI and LLM applications, supported by AI/ML, computer vision and full-stack engineering.

### Audience and first-minute priorities

Primary readers are technical recruiters, AI/ML hiring teams, software engineering recruiters and internship coordinators. Founders and potential collaborators are a secondary audience. In the first screen they should be able to answer:

1. Who is Sandesh and where is he based?
2. What roles is he pursuing? (Paid AI / GenAI / ML internships; related engineering opportunities.)
3. What has he built? (Lead with ChestVision AI and Product Return Intelligence.)
4. Where can they inspect the work and contact him?

Visual intrigue must never obscure these answers. Keep a visible “Selected work” route, GitHub, LinkedIn, email and a clearly labeled résumé link when the actual résumé file is supplied.

## 2. Three visual concepts

### A. The Atlas of Becoming — **recommended**

An editorial field atlas that combines deep ocean ink, warm paper, terracotta indexing and measured technical annotations. Use true portraits and product interfaces as evidence. Thin route lines connect disciplines and projects. A restrained 3D “survey object” or layered topographic relief can sit behind the hero, with a static illustration fallback.

**Why it wins:** It unites exploration, architecture, experimentation and identity without implying seniority. It can hold both serious AI systems and the human story. It supports clear recruiter navigation and gives the supplied photographs and polished product screenshots a common framing device.

### B. The Monsoon Field Notes

A warmer, more photographic journal shaped around landscape, weather, travel and the act of noticing. Rain, wet foliage, coastal horizon and cultivated greenery become transitions between chapters; hand-annotated coordinates and small technical captions hold the engineering material together. A good route if personal travel imagery becomes a stronger part of the story.

**Trade-off:** It could tilt toward a travel journal and soften the technical signal. Keep projects and role information close to the top.

### C. The Systems Observatory

A darker, more architectural direction: midnight surfaces, precise grids, radar-like rings and restrained data visualization. It frames projects as instruments for observing a problem and makes model pipelines and interfaces prominent.

**Trade-off:** It risks feeling like a generic AI dashboard or overplaying clinical/enterprise visual language. Use only as a secondary layer for case studies, never as the entire identity.

**Recommendation:** Use A as the main system, borrowing B’s real-world warmth in the About and personal chapter, and C’s technical precision within project case studies.

## 3. Moodboard and visual language

Imagine an open field atlas on a dark desk: a cream paper page marked with fine coordinates; a copper-red route crosses a muted teal contour; an architectural object catches soft, directional light; a sharply cropped, genuine portrait interrupts the abstraction; a project screen appears as a clean, legible evidence plate. The page has generous space, crisp editorial type and small mono labels such as `FIELD NOTE 01`, `PIMPRI-CHINCHWAD / INDIA`, `MODEL → INTERFACE`, and `2024—2028`.

### Visual ingredients

- Architectural geometry, not ornamental geometry: section cuts, perspective frames, a grid, registration marks and contour lines.
- Tactile atmosphere in restrained doses: paper grain, stone or brushed-metal references, photographic shadow, subtle scan texture. Avoid full-page noise overlays.
- One recurring route motif: a thin line, node or plotted coordinate that links chapters and projects. It is a navigation cue, not a logo.
- A photographic contrast: honest, naturally lit personal images next to highly composed product imagery.
- A clear distinction between **story** (larger serif / photographic moments) and **engineering evidence** (sans headings, mono metadata, UI screenshots, diagrams).
- No personal logo, name-mark or invented monogram. The name is plain typography in the navigation.

## 6. Typography system

Use three families with defined jobs. This keeps the system expressive without making every section look like a different template.

| Role | Typeface | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Display / interface sans | **Space Grotesk** | Strong, modern engineering voice; hero, section titles, navigation |
| Editorial accent | **Instrument Serif** | Selective contrast for short reflective phrases and story pull-quotes |
| Technical metadata | **IBM Plex Mono** | Coordinates, dates, tags, model facts and captions |

Recommended weights: Space Grotesk 400, 500, 600, 700; Instrument Serif regular and italic; IBM Plex Mono 400, 500. Self-host optimized WOFF2 subsets if licensing and language coverage permit. Load only essential weights; avoid a remote-font dependency that blocks first paint.

### Scale and hierarchy (desktop starting values)

- Hero: 72–112 px, tightly led, preferably 2–3 intentional lines; responsive clamp down to 44–56 px on small screens.
- Chapter title: 48–72 px; one clear idea, no decorative microtext competing with it.
- Project title: 32–44 px.
- Body: 17–19 px with 1.55–1.7 line height; keep measure around 60–72 characters.
- Caption / metadata: 12–14 px. Never put important context in tiny mono labels.
- Navigation: 14–16 px, medium weight.

Use italics and serif only to mark reflection, not every second word. Avoid ultra-condensed body text, outlined display letters, all-caps paragraphs and excessive tracking. Keep mobile heading wraps deliberate and body copy comfortable at 16 px or larger.

## 9. Motion and interaction direction

Motion should feel like a page opening, a map being plotted and an image being uncovered. Use the least complex tool that produces the intended effect. The later implementation brief may choose GSAP with ScrollTrigger for a few coordinated reveals and CSS for simple states. Do not install GSAP, Framer Motion, Anime.js, Lenis, Locomotive and Three.js all at once. No simultaneous transform ownership by multiple animation systems.

| Moment | Visitor sees / trigger | Purpose | Suggested tool | Priority / fallback |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Entry | The portrait and headline are already visible; a short route stroke draws in once, then settles. | Establish direction without hiding the page behind a loader. | CSS or GSAP timeline | Essential, under ~700 ms; reduced motion shows final state immediately. |
| Hero typography | Headline lines rise behind a subtle mask on first view, once. | Editorial emphasis. | GSAP + useGSAP or CSS | Essential only if it leaves text in HTML and does not delay reading. |
| Portrait reveal | A restrained clipped edge opens to the real photo. | Introduce person and image. | CSS/GSAP | Optional; image visible without script. |
| Route navigation | Active chapter dot and line update as sections enter view. | Orientation across long scroll. | IntersectionObserver, or ScrollTrigger if already used | Useful; keep names and anchor links usable without JS. |
| Section reveal | Image / heading moves by a few pixels and fades in once. | Pace the page, not decorate every paragraph. | CSS IntersectionObserver or ScrollTrigger | Optional; visible by default, no hidden content if script fails. |
| Project hover | Image shifts slightly or a terracotta frame appears; title and “Read case study” remain clear. | Signal clickability. | CSS; Framer Motion only if app-level layout state is needed | Essential micro-feedback; keyboard focus equivalent. |
| Case-study diagrams | Pipeline nodes illuminate in order as the diagram enters view. | Show system sequence. | CSS/GSAP timeline | Optional; static labeled diagram is fallback. |
| Personal chapter | Rain/field photo drifts by only a few percent relative to scroll. | Add breathing room and memory. | GSAP ScrollTrigger | Optional, desktop only; no smoothing dependency. |
| Contact | Link arrow nudges on hover/focus; email becomes a mailto action. | Confirm action. | CSS | Essential micro-feedback. |

### Scroll experience

Keep native page scrolling. Prefer native anchor behavior and a small persistent chapter index that collapses on mobile. Lenis is not required for a long editorial page; use it only if a tested prototype clearly improves comfort, and keep keyboard, touch, anchor and back/forward behavior native-feeling. Do not pair Lenis with Locomotive as separate engines. No long pinned section that traps the visitor; any horizontal project gallery must have a clear vertical/mobile alternative and accessible controls.

### Preloader

Do not use a blocking preloader. The master requirements mention one, but the performance requirement says meaningful content must appear immediately and must not wait for 3D. Reconcile them with an in-page, non-blocking “route drawing” micro-animation after content paint, or omit it. If an ornamental loader remains, it must dismiss quickly, respect `prefers-reduced-motion`, and never gate reading or interaction.

### Reduced motion and keyboard

For `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, disable parallax, smooth-scroll enhancements, animated text splitting, auto-playing sequences and nonessential transitions. Present complete final content, preserve focus order, ensure every hover action also works on keyboard focus, and avoid mouse-only custom cursors.
